Point of View

The Case for a Two-Phase Approach

Truist's 4.7 headline rating masks a structural issue: its normalized complaint rate in checking and savings is the highest in the peer set. That is a direct consequence of the BB&T / SunTrust merger, where two digital platforms were integrated rather than rebuilt. Chase and BofA used similar consolidation periods to redesign the core experience; Truist did not.

The two-phase case is straightforward. Phase one removes friction that is generating measurable complaints today — auth loops, deposit holds, Zelle disputes. These are fixable in 2-3 quarters without major architectural changes. Phase two builds the differentiated layer. Fifth Third won J.D. Power's regional bank ranking in 2025 by targeting the same friction points. Truist has a larger asset base and an existing wealth management data advantage it has not yet surfaced in the app.

38% of Truist complaints are in checking/savings, highest in peer set
3x auth-related mention rate vs Chase in 1-star reviews
0 in-app AI assistant features vs BofA's Erica (2B+ interactions/yr)

Methodology

CFPB Data

Pulled via consumerfinance.gov/data-research/consumer-complaints/search/api/v1. Exact company-name matching per CFPB entity. Deposit figures from FDIC Summary of Deposits, Q1 2026.

App Store Data

iOS and Google Play rating distributions. Review theme clustering based on keyword frequency in 1-3 star text. Cross-validated against J.D. Power 2025-2026 Mobile Banking Satisfaction studies.

RICE Scoring

Reach = estimated annual Truist users affected, derived from complaint share x active user base. Impact 1-5 by churn signal strength. Confidence = data quality estimate. Effort in person-quarters.

Competitive Set

National peers: BofA, Chase, Wells Fargo. Regional peers: PNC, Fifth Third. Deposit data from FDIC 2025.
